Write a short-form video package for the idea below, following the guide above.

## My voice (edit these: paste in hooks you have really said)

Plain, a bit cheeky, first person, like telling a mate what I built this week. Short sentences. No hype words, no "game-changer", no "unlock", no "in today's video". Real hooks of mine, for the feel only (never reuse them):

- "I've Clauded so hard, I built a Bible widget in my dashboard."
- "Five cool features I built into my personal dashboard without using a line of code."

## Evidence (when a WHAT HAS WORKED block is given below)

- W lines are my own hooks that beat my usual numbers. Lean towards what they have in common (the angle, the length, how specific they are). Do not repeat one.
- O lines are structures from other creators' outliers. Borrow the shape and the order of beats only. Never their wording, never name them.
- If there is no block, or it is thin, write from the idea alone. Never invent evidence.

## Hooks: 5 options

Each under 12 words, each from a different formula in the guide, and each one has to pass all of these:

1. One concrete detail from this idea: a named thing on screen, a specific moment, a real object. "A quote that chases itself" beats "an automation for quotes".
2. An open loop: the viewer must want the next 5 seconds to find out how, why or what happened. Do not give the answer away in the hook.
3. Sayable in one breath, in the words a business owner would use at the counter. No jargon.
4. The five must be genuinely different angles, not the same line reworded.
5. Never open with: "Did you know", "Are you tired of", "In this video", "In today's video", "Hey guys", "Welcome back", "Let me show you", "What if I told you", "Ever wondered", "Here's how".

favourite_hook: the one most likely to stop a scroll for this audience. Say it out loud first: if it sounds like an advert, pick another.

## Script: 30-45 seconds spoken (about 80-110 words)

- Line 1 is the favourite hook, word for word.
- Line 2 raises the stakes or sharpens the question. It does not answer it yet.
- Then the problem in one picture the viewer has lived (a specific moment, not a general complaint).
- Then the thing working on screen, in the order a viewer would watch it happen. Say what they are seeing.
- Pay off the hook: answer the open loop plainly before the call to action.
- Write for the ear: contractions, short lines, one thought per line, a new line for each beat. No lists of three adjectives.
- Cut any sentence the video would survive without.

## Call to action: pick the one that fits, never bolt one on

The last line follows from the payoff, as the obvious next step for someone who just watched it. Choose one cta_type:

- comment: the video shows something they could have (a build, template, checklist). "Comment KEYWORD and I'll send you the build." The keyword is one uppercase word tied to the video.
- follow: the video is part of a series or build in public. Tease the specific next thing: "Follow, tomorrow I'm wiring this to the calendar."
- save: the video teaches steps they will want again. "Save this for the next time a quote goes quiet."
- dm: the video describes a problem they may have right now. "If that's your Monday morning, message me 'QUOTES'."

Rules for it: one ask only, under 14 words, said the way I talk, and specific to this video (a line that would fit any video is wrong). Give the viewer a reason, not an order.

## Provide

- hooks: the 5 options
- favourite_hook: the best of the five, copied exactly
- script: the spoken script, ending with the call to action
- on_screen_text: one short line per beat, plus what to screen-record; the last line is the end card showing the call to action
- cta_type: comment, follow, save or dm
- cta_line: the call to action exactly as spoken
- caption: under 60 words, opens with a line that is not the hook repeated, and ends with the call to action
- keyword: one uppercase word when cta_type is comment or dm (the word they send); otherwise an empty string

Rules: British English. Only claim results that are true; do not invent clients, numbers or testimonials. If the idea needs a result you can't know, show the build working instead. Business data only, never a client's customers' details, and never name a real client or business.

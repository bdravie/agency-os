## A fresh angle, in words nobody else is using (the app checks this and refuses the draft if it fails)

Most people posting to this audience say the same things in the same words. This post must not read like theirs.

Before you write, do this in your head (do not write your thinking out; only the finished result is submitted):

1. Name the post nearly everyone in this niche would write on this subject: the obvious take and the obvious wording. That is the post you are NOT writing. Put it in `expected_take`.
2. Find the angle they miss, from the source itself. Good places to look: one exact moment (a time, a place, an object in the room); the view of someone who is usually left out (the person on hold, the receptionist with a queue at the desk, the builder on site); a cost nobody counts; the step everybody skips; something the owner tried and what happened; the opposite of the usual advice, when the source backs it. Put it in `angle`, in one sentence.
3. Write the whole post from that angle. Every paragraph should be something only this post could say.

Wording:

- If a sentence could sit unchanged in a competitor's post, or in any agency's post, replace it with a detail from the source.
- No stock constructions: "X isn't the problem. Y is.", "It's not about X, it's about Y", "It isn't here to replace your team, it's here to support them", "Picture this", "Sound familiar?", "Here's the thing", a rhetorical question answered in the next breath, or three adjectives in a row.
- The hook formulas in the guide are shapes to think with, never words to copy. Do not start a hook with the formula's own words ("Steal this", "Stop doing", "If you run a", "Before:").
- Use the words the reader would say out loud at work, not the words a marketer would write about them.

Never the same phrase in two posts:

- The ALREADY USED block below lists lines from earlier posts. Do not reuse them, their openings or their closing lines, and do not lightly reword them.
- When the same point has to be made again (the missed call, the slow reply, the enquiry nobody followed up), make it from a different angle: another moment, another person's view, another picture. Changing two words is not a different angle.
- This includes the call to action. Ask for the same thing in a new way each time, tied to this post.
- Wording in the SOURCE that came from another post (a video's hook or script) counts as already used: carry the idea across, not the sentences.

Each place has its own way of speaking:

- **Instagram**: the caption sits under the video, so it does not retell it. Open with something the video leaves out (a behind-the-scenes detail, why it was made, what went wrong the first time). Warm and conversational, like a message to someone who already follows.
- **TikTok**: shorter and blunter than Instagram, lower case in spirit, one thought. Sound like a person mid-conversation, never a brand. A different opening line and a different closing ask from the Instagram caption.
- **LinkedIn**: written for an owner or manager reading at a desk. One idea argued properly, from experience, with the reasoning shown. No motivational tone and no announcement voice.

The same video on two platforms is two posts: they share the idea, never the sentences.

Write one LinkedIn text post from the SOURCE below, following the guide above. The owner posts it by hand.

## My voice (edit these: paste in opening lines you have really posted)

Plain, first person, a bit cheeky, like telling another business owner what I noticed this week. Short sentences. No hype words, no "game-changer", no "unlock", no "I'm thrilled to announce", no "Let that sink in". Real lines of mine, for the feel only (never reuse them):

- "I've Clauded so hard, I built a Bible widget in my dashboard."
- "Five cool features I built into my personal dashboard without using a line of code."

## Who reads it

Owners and managers of the businesses named in the guide, scrolling LinkedIn between jobs. They decide in the first two lines whether to tap "see more". They care about enquiries, booked work, money kept and hours back, not about tools.

## Evidence (when a WHAT HAS WORKED block is given below)

- W lines are my own openings that beat my usual numbers. Lean towards what they have in common. Do not repeat one.
- O lines are structures from other creators' posts. Borrow the shape and the order only. Never their wording, never name them.
- If there is no block, write from the source alone. Never invent evidence.

## Opening lines: 3 options (hooks)

Each is one sentence of 16 words or fewer, each a different angle, and each must pass all of these:

1. One concrete detail from the source: a specific moment, a named thing, a real object. "The quote sat in a van for three days" beats "quotes get delayed".
2. An open loop: the reader must want the next line to find out how, why or what happened.
3. Makes sense on its own, because it is all most people will see.
4. Never open with: "Did you know", "Are you tired of", "In this video", "Hey guys", "Let me show you", "What if I told you", "Ever wondered", "Here's how".

favourite_hook: the one most likely to earn the tap, copied word for word from the three.

## Body: 110 to 200 words (the opening line is added in front by the app, so do not repeat it)

- The first line of the body sharpens the opening. It does not answer it yet.
- Then the problem as one picture the reader has lived.
- Then what was changed or built, in the order it happens, in plain words. A short list of 3 to 5 lines is fine when the source has steps; start each with a dash.
- Pay the opening off plainly before the end.
- End with one question the reader can answer from their own business, or one plain next step. Never "Thoughts?" and never "Agree?".
- Short paragraphs of one or two sentences with a blank line between them. Written to be read on a phone.
- No emoji. No bold or italics. No headings.

## Hashtags

Up to 3, single words or joined words a business owner in that trade would follow, without the # sign. None is fine.

## Hard rules (the app checks these and refuses the post if one is broken)

- No money amounts, percentages or multipliers, and no result that is not stated as a fact in the source. Say what a change is designed to do.
- Do not write about "my clients" or what clients got: none were supplied.
- No links, no @ names, no email addresses, no phone numbers.
- Never name a business or a person. Describe the pattern ("a roofing firm", "most dental sites I looked at").
- Business data only: never anything about a business's own customers or patients as individuals.
- If the source is about a demo, call it a demo.
- British English.

title: a working title of 3 to 8 words for the owner's own list. It is never posted.

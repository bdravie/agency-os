# How the consultant describes what they do (read this first)

Lead with the result for the business, never the technology. The consultant helps small businesses get more leads, win more of the work that comes in, make more money from what they already do and get hours back, by improving the systems behind the business.

- Pick the ONE result that fits the reader: more enquiries, more of them turned into booked work, fewer quotes and customers slipping away, less admin time, clearer numbers.
- Say what is improved in plain business terms: how enquiries are answered, how quotes are followed up, how reviews are asked for, how jobs, bookings and takings are tracked and reported.
- The technology (AI, automation, tools, code) is only the method. Mention it briefly, or not at all, and only when it helps the reader trust the result.
- Do not describe the consultant as "building AI automations", an "AI automation" business or an "AI consultant", and do not lead with tool names or jargon (workflow, pipeline, LLM, agentic).
- Be honest about results. Describe what a change is designed to do ("so fewer enquiries go cold", "so you get your evenings back"), never what it has done for someone, unless the facts supplied say so. Never promise or imply figures, growth, a number of leads or an amount saved that was not supplied as a true fact.
- Write for a busy owner, not a technologist: short, plain British English.

Agency OS · brand sheet · draft v1 for review

Calm paper, one ember to follow.

A light, warm workspace where white does the resting and orange marks the one thing that needs you. A deep teal means "done" and "on track". Everything on this page is live: hover, click, press Replay.

The 60 / 30 / 10 rule

Mostly paper and white. Orange is the action and the current focus. Teal appears only when something is finished or healthy. If a screen looks orange all over, it is wrong.

02 · Colour

White, Ember and Lagoon

Your two colours are white and orange. For the third I chose Lagoon, a deep teal. It sits opposite orange on the colour wheel, so both look stronger next to each other. It also reads naturally as "done", "on track" and "money kept", which frees orange to mean only "look here". It is far from the video's cream, black and green, which keeps this yours.

05 · Motion

Six motions, each with a job

Rule: motion explains a change; it never decorates. Everything is 150 to 450ms with one easing curve (cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1)), and switches off for anyone with "reduce motion" set.

09 · How every new feature follows this

The checklist

Always

  • Use tokens (--ember, --lagoon, --card…) and never a raw hex.
  • White cards on Paper, Sand tiles inside, 20px / 14px / 10px radii, shadows instead of borders.
  • One Ember button per screen; ink for secondary; outlined for the rest.
  • Big numbers in Bricolage with tabular figures; labels in small capitals.
  • Progress shows fill plus a pace tick; finished turns Lagoon.
  • Success is a button morph, not a pop-up. Respect "reduce motion".
  • Every page works with demo data and looks good on camera.

Never

  • White text on Ember (use Ink), or Ember text smaller than 14px (use the darker Ember text).
  • Orange backgrounds on whole sections, or more than one Ember button in view.
  • Red for decoration; teal for anything not finished or healthy.
  • Looping or decorative animation, or anything slower than 450ms.
  • New fonts, new accent colours or gradients.
  • Copying another product's layout or colours.

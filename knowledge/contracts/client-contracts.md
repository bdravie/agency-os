# Client contracts: what to send and when

Starter notes for a UK business-to-business systems and automation agency. They are a checklist, not legal advice: have a solicitor review each template once, then keep using the same templates and record any changes they make here. The law is England and Wales unless a client insists otherwise.

## The order documents usually go out
1. **Mutual NDA** (optional): before an audit or discovery call where the client will show you sensitive figures or systems.
2. **Proposal or quote**: what you will do, the price and what it assumes. A quote the client accepts in writing can form a contract on its own, so it should point to your terms.
3. **Terms of business (a master services agreement)**: signed once per client; covers every project and retainer that follows.
4. **Statement of work (SOW)**: one per project, under the terms of business.
5. **Data processing agreement (DPA)**: before you build anything that handles personal data on the client's behalf. The CRM's own rule: no work that touches a client's customers' or patients' personal data until a DPA and DPIA are in place.
6. **Retainer or support agreement**: when a build turns into monthly optimisation or support.
7. **Change request**: whenever scope, price or dates change mid-project.
8. **Sign-off (acceptance) note**: when a milestone or the project is delivered.
9. **Case study and testimonial permission**: written approval before you name the client or use their results anywhere.

## Terms of business: what to cover
- **Scope by SOW**: the terms say work is defined in each SOW, so the terms never need rewriting.
- **Fees and payment**: deposit (many agencies take 50% upfront on builds), when invoices fall due (for example 14 or 30 days), and that work may pause on unpaid invoices.
- **Late payment**: in business-to-business contracts the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 lets you claim statutory interest (8% above the Bank of England base rate) and a fixed sum per invoice (£40, £70 or £100 depending on the debt) if the contract does not set its own substantial remedy. The government consulted on tougher late-payment rules in 2025, so check gov.uk for the current position.
- **Client responsibilities**: access to accounts and data on time, a named decision-maker, prompt feedback. Delays on their side move your dates.
- **Third-party tools**: the client owns and pays for their own subscriptions (automation platform, AI provider, CRM, hosting) and those providers' terms apply. You are not liable for a third party's outage or price change.
- **AI output**: anything an AI step produces is a draft for the client's people to review before use; the client decides what is sent to their customers.
- **Intellectual property**: what you build for them passes to them (or is licensed) on full payment; you keep your pre-existing tools, templates and know-how and may reuse them; you may show the work as a demo with their data removed, only with permission to name them.
- **Warranties**: services carried out with reasonable care and skill (the standard the Supply of Goods and Services Act 1982 already implies), and a short fix period for defects after sign-off.
- **Limitation of liability**: a cap (commonly the fees paid in the previous 12 months) and no liability for indirect loss or lost profits. You cannot exclude liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, or for fraud, and other exclusions in standard terms must be reasonable under the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977.
- **Confidentiality** both ways, surviving the end of the contract.
- **Data protection**: a clause saying a DPA applies whenever you process personal data for them.
- **Termination**: notice period for retainers (for example 30 days), immediate termination for material breach or insolvency, payment for work done to date.
- **Non-solicitation** (optional) and **governing law**: England and Wales.

## Statement of work: what to cover
- The problem and the result it is designed to achieve, in business terms (never a guaranteed figure).
- Deliverables and what is out of scope.
- Milestones with dates, and what the client must provide for each.
- Acceptance criteria and a sign-off window (for example: deemed accepted if no written issues within 5 working days).
- Assumptions (volumes, tools, access) and what happens if they prove wrong: a change request.
- Price, payment schedule and which party pays which subscriptions.

## Data processing agreement (UK GDPR Article 28)
Needed when you process personal data **on the client's behalf**: they are the controller, you are the processor. Article 28(3) requires the contract to say that you will:
1. process the data only on the client's documented instructions;
2. make sure anyone handling it is bound by confidentiality;
3. keep it secure (Article 32 measures: access control, encryption where sensible, backups, least privilege);
4. use sub-processors only with the client's authorisation, on the same terms, and stay responsible for them;
5. help the client answer people exercising their data rights;
6. help the client with security, breach notification, DPIAs and consulting the ICO (Articles 32 to 36);
7. delete or return the data at the end of the contract;
8. give the client the information needed to show compliance, and allow audits.
Also list: the subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of the processing, the types of data and people, every sub-processor (hosting, automation platform, AI provider) with where it processes data, how international transfers are covered (UK adequacy regulations, the IDTA, or the UK Addendum to the EU standard clauses), and how fast you tell the client about a breach (without undue delay; the client has 72 hours to tell the ICO where required).

The **DPIA** (data protection impact assessment) is the client's to carry out as controller when processing is likely to be high risk; you help with how the system works. Health data (clinics) is special category data, so treat clinic builds as needing a DPIA. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 changed parts of UK data protection law, so check the ICO's current guidance before relying on a template.

## Retainer or support agreement
What the monthly fee includes (hours, monitoring, changes per month), response times as targets not guarantees, whether unused hours roll over, the price review date, and the notice period.

## Practical points
- Electronic signatures are valid for these contracts in England and Wales; an e-signature tool with an audit trail is enough.
- Keep signed copies against the company in the CRM (Deals & work, or the documents area) so they are easy to find.
- Before any recorded call, the invitation says it will be recorded; calls are only added to the CRM when everyone knew.
- Check whether you need to pay the ICO data protection fee and keep it current.

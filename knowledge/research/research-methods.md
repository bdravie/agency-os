# Research methods

How to research niches, companies and content inside the business rules (CLAUDE.md). The assistant can also search the web for a question when Search the web is ticked; it then cites the pages it used.

## What is allowed to be collected
- Companies, not people: company name, website, phone, address, ratings, generic emails (info@, hello@, contact@, enquiries@) and company social pages.
- Named people only once they have engaged (filled in a form, booked, replied, messaged, became a client), or as senior decision-makers through the approved routes (Apollo, a manual export, or pasting people found by hand) for verified active limited companies, with the lawful basis recorded.
- Public sources only, robots.txt respected, within each tool's free caps. Never social platforms for research on people, never people-finder sites, never review text.
- Outreach only to limited companies (Companies House status active, type ltd, llp or plc) that pass the lead gate.

## Researching a niche
1. **Who pays and why**: what a lost customer, a missed call or an admin hour costs a business in this niche, in their words.
2. **How they get work today**: directories, referrals, Google Maps, social, marketplaces. Look at ten businesses' websites and booking flows.
3. **Where the time goes**: the repeated admin jobs (enquiries, booking, reminders, quotes, follow-ups, reporting) and which ones touch their customers' personal data (those need a data processing agreement first).
4. **Who decides**: owner, practice manager, operations lead. Note job titles, not names.
5. **Proof you can show**: which demo in `demos/` maps to their pain, and what you would need to build to show it.
6. Record what you learn in this folder (one file per niche) so the assistant can use it next time.

## Researching one company before contact
- Read only its own website (the CRM's Offer suggestion does this on click, up to four pages) and note business observations in Research notes.
- Check Companies House status and type before anything else; an unverified company stays blocked.
- Look for one genuine, specific detail to mention (a service they push, a booking step that is slow), never something about a person's private life.

## Market and competitor research
- Search the way a customer would ("emergency dentist near me", "botox clinic Leeds") and note who appears, what they promise and how you book.
- Compare three competitors on the same three things: how fast you can book, what happens after hours, what follow-up you get.
- Upwork and job boards show what businesses already pay for: collect job titles and the tools named, not the posters.

## Content research
- Creators: add them by hand on Content > Research > Creators; only public profile and post numbers are kept.
- Outliers (posts at three times or more a creator's median views) show which structures work; break one down and borrow the structure and angle, never the wording.
- Trends: keep titles and links on Content > Research > Trends.

## Writing up research
- Separate what you saw (with the source) from what you think it means.
- Date every finding; niches change.
- Prefer counts you can check ("7 of 10 sites had no online booking") to adjectives.

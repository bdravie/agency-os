An aesthetics clinic gets enquiries in more places than almost any other business: phone, website form, email and direct messages on social media. Most of them arrive while you are with a client, with gloves on, and cannot answer.

That is the whole problem in one sentence. The rest is detail.

## An enquiry is a mood, not a ticket

Someone who messages about a consultation has usually been thinking about it for a while. The message is the moment they decided to act. Reply in that moment and you are continuing a conversation they started. Reply the next day and you are restarting one they may have already had with another clinic.

Nothing about your clinic got worse overnight. The timing did.

## Why the reply is late

It is rarely carelessness. It is usually one of these:

- **You are treating.** The person who knows the answers is the person who cannot pick up the phone.
- **Enquiries are spread about.** A message in one app, a voicemail, a form: nobody has one list of who is waiting.
- **The reply is not a booking.** "Thanks, when suits you?" starts a back-and-forth that can take days, and each round is a chance to drift.

## What a good first reply does

It does not need to be clever. It needs to do two things quickly: tell the person they have been heard, and offer a consultation time they can say yes to.

It should also stay in its lane. A first reply is about booking. Questions about treatments, suitability or medicines are for the consultation itself, with a qualified person, never for a quick message.

## Three things to check this week

1. **Count the places an enquiry can arrive,** and ask who looks at each one, and when.
2. **Look at yesterday's enquiries** and note how long each waited for a first reply.
3. **Read your last few first replies.** Did they offer a time, or ask a question that needed another reply?

If one channel turns out to be the slow one, start there. Closing one gap properly is worth more than half-closing four.

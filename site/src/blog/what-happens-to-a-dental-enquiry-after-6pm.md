Somebody chips a tooth at dinner. They are not in pain, but they want it looked at, so they search for a dentist nearby and get in touch with the first practice that looks right. It is twenty to seven. You closed at six.

What happens next is worth walking through, because it is the same at most practices and nobody designed it that way.

## The evening

If they phoned, they heard your out-of-hours message. Some people leave a voicemail. Plenty do not: they hang up and try the next practice on the list.

If they filled in the form on your website, they got a line saying you will be in touch. Nothing else happens tonight.

Either way, the person has done their part. They wanted an appointment and they asked for one.

## The morning

Your receptionist arrives to a waiting room, a ringing phone and the day's first patients. The voicemails and the form enquiries are in a queue behind all of that.

This is not a staffing failure. The desk is doing the right thing by looking after the people who are there. But it means the evening enquiry is answered mid-morning at the earliest, and by then the person may have booked with whoever replied first.

## Where it actually goes missing

It is tempting to think the enquiry is lost overnight. It is more useful to see three separate gaps:

- **No answer at the moment they asked.** The person was ready to book at twenty to seven.
- **No appointment offered.** "We will be in touch" is a promise of more waiting.
- **A queue at the worst time.** The reply depends on a quiet moment at the busiest hour of the day.

## Three things to check this week

You can find out how big the problem is without buying anything.

1. **Ring your own practice after closing.** Listen to the message as a new patient would. Does it tell them what to do next?
2. **Send your own website form in the evening.** Note when the reply arrives and whether it offers a time.
3. **Ask the front desk how many voicemails and forms are waiting on a normal morning,** and how long it takes to clear them.

If the answers are "it is fine", good, and you have lost nothing by checking. If they are not, you now know which of the three gaps to close first.
